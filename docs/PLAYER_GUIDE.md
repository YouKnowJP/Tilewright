# Welcome to Lantern Trails

The woods have lost their light. You can follow a story, set off on a longer
journey, or make a world for someone else to discover.

## Pip’s invention workshop

Choose **Play Pip & Sprout** on the main menu. Start with **Begin the story**,
continue a saved setup, or browse any of forty-four chapters. **Find an
adventure** filters by region, progress, title or mechanic. No chapter is locked.

Use **Cards** to set Pip and Sprout's three repeating instructions, then
**Garden** to return to the board. **Test invention** starts your route. Step
once, advance to an event, Run or Undo. **Tools** appears when a repair or
creator garden has parts to configure. **Briefing & progress** holds the longer
story and personal bests; **Hints** offers a small clue, an experiment and an
optional worked route. Loading an example asks before replacing the draft.

With the garden focused: **Space/Enter** steps a test, **R** runs/pauses and **Z**
undoes. Arrows or your saved WASD/IJKL layout inspect tiles. At the bench,
Space/Enter places or inspects. Escape pauses a running test; press again to
leave. Playback also pauses when the window or page is away. Choose Run yourself
when you return. Menus keep controller input inside the current choice.

**Try a tiny practice garden** opens a separate handoff, bell or passport lesson.
Choose one next card per friend and advance them together, or step through a
guided run. Undo/reset are free. Returning leaves your adventure where it was;
practice does not award clears or write over your cards.

**Your collections** opens saved chapters or resumes a saved test with its Undo
history. A collection played with a full shelf is labelled **This visit** and
remains available through Adventure Home while the page stays open. Free a shelf
slot and save the collection to keep its progress between visits. A collection
download keeps the authored chapters. Ordinary story continuation
restores cards and parts. **Create & share**
opens your garden, collection builder or project import. **How to play** includes
the classic single-Pip lessons. Adventure Home returns to the main menu.
Full journey backups preserve creations, collections and personal progress.
See the [complete workshop guide](PIP_WORKSHOP.md) for all mechanics and tools.

## The Clockwork Gardens — v0.41

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Clockwork Gardens**.
Six chapters teach returning signs, a different starting route, repeated parcel
trips, shared bridge timing and a four-road courier braid. The final garden
combines three remembered routes, two recipes, two jade deliveries, a crate
bridge and a bell duet. Scout offers a complete route when you want help.

**Z** is a flip stamp. Pip's Use loads **Route A**, then **Route B** on its next
visit, alternating thereafter. Some signs start on B. Each sign remembers its
own next route, keeps Pip's parcel and spends no keeper rewrite. Its selected
program stays loaded until changed; the next turn begins with card 1.
Walking or waiting beside a sign does not flip it. Route hardware lists both
programs, the next route and which routes have been used. Quest may require
both. Undo and Continue keep the memories; an allowed dock return restores
each sign's starting route and clears its route goals.

Build one under **Workshop → Puzzles → Pip flip stamp**. Engrave both programs,
optionally start on B and require both routes. No pressure signal or parcel
source is needed for the sign itself. In the final garden, the signs remember
their turns while the parcel waits for the right recipe; a wrong recipe keeps it.

Open **Puzzle assemblies → Workshop blueprints** for four original building
pieces. Choose one to see its original map and builder note, then **Keep a copy**
to add a new personal slot. Copies can be renamed, mirrored, rotated, downloaded
and placed like your own pieces. The current world changes only when you place
a piece; Undo restores that placement. Copying never replaces an existing slot.
Read the new link numbers after placement, and playtest your full adventure
before sharing its verified file, code, link or Storybook.

## The Glassworks — v0.40

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Glassworks**. Six
chapters grow from one recipe into return trips, a two-crossing ferry and a
four-window factory supplied by one amber well. Scout gives a complete route;
Start/Pause and Advance one card both work.

**E** is a parcel press. Pip uses it to turn one matching input parcel into its
output. Empty hands or a different parcel stay unchanged. A press keeps the
normal card order and spends no rewrite. Some recipes need several successful
conversions; failed attempts do not count. Completed presses keep working.
**Route hardware** shows both parcel numbers/names and the successful-use count.
Quest includes required recipes. Undo and Continue retain the parcel and counts;
an allowed dock return clears them along with receiver power.

Build recipes in **Workshop → Puzzles → Pip parcel press**. Set different input
and output parcels, a one-to-four successful-use target and whether the recipe
is required. Include a source and a path of recipes to a receiver. Recipes can
feed junctions and other presses, including useful return cycles.

In **Puzzle assemblies**, **Edit details** changes a saved piece's name and
builder note. Save details keeps the edit; Cancel edit leaves the original.
The current mirror/turn preview stays selected. Download puzzle shares that
preview, while its saved original keeps its geometry. Capture the complete
recipe network so its input and output links stay together. Share a verified
adventure link or Storybook when your friend wants to play immediately.

## Switchback Station — v0.39

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Switchback Station**.
Six chapters teach parcel questions, returning refill roads, wrong-colour
branches, cooperative crossings and a four-colour station.

**J** is a parcel junction. Pip’s Use reads the parcel it carries. A matching
parcel loads **Matching**; empty hands or another colour load **Otherwise**.
Reading keeps the parcel, starts the selected program at card 1 next turn,
and spends no keeper rewrite. The chosen program stays loaded until changed.
Junctions read cargo; M signal stamps still read plates, levers and charged
receivers. Route hardware shows the current answer, both programs and which
answers you have used. Some worlds require reading both answers.

Start/Pause and Advance one card both work. Pause safely at a junction before
changing a bridge plate. In the finale, release jade after the amber platform
is finished so the jade counting stamp can read Quiet. Scout gives a complete
route when you want help. Undo and Restart remain available.

Creators can paint **Pip parcel junction** under Puzzles, choose its parcel
channel and engrave both programs. **Swap the two programs** edits the draft;
Apply object keeps it. In Puzzle assemblies, optional builder notes travel
with downloaded pieces, imports, mirrors and rotations. Notes explain a piece
to another builder; add a keeper or story moment for instructions during play.

## The Lantern Foundry — v0.38

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Lantern Foundry**.
Six chapters introduce power parcels, then build through returning sources,
coordinated crossings and a four-colour freight route.

Pip uses **Q** to collect a parcel and matching **N** to deliver it. It carries
one parcel at a time. Sources can refill its hands after a delivery; trying to
collect another while carrying one keeps the original parcel. A full receiver
consumes nothing. Some receivers need two or three deliveries. Every receiver
on a channel must reach its target before its parcel crossings open or its
signal stamps read Powered. Existing levers and pressure plates can also power
signal stamps. Tubes and prisms carry the parcel safely.

The companion workbench shows Pip’s current parcel. **Route hardware** lists
source links and receiver counts; Quest lists required receivers. Deliveries
stay powered while Pip walks elsewhere. Undo restores the parcel and counts.
An allowed dock return clears them without refilling rewrites, and refuses if
it would close a parcel crossing beneath a traveler or crate. The Foundry
trials seal the dock. Rehearse for free before applying your program.

Creators can paint **Pip parcel source**, **Pip parcel receiver**, and **Parcel
crossing** in Puzzles. Choose matching linked channels, set receiver targets,
and test the full return trip. In **Puzzle assemblies**, choose a reflection
and rotation to see a live preview. Placement and **Download puzzle** use that
preview; the saved original stays unchanged. Imported preview files already
contain their displayed orientation, so choose No reflection / Original if you
want to place them as downloaded.

## The Prism Observatory — v0.37

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Prism Observatory**.
Six chapters teach compass prisms, marked **X**, then build toward a four-wind
circuit, a four-bell tube relay and an eight-prism finale.

Pip executes **Use** on a prism to transform its current cards. A quarter turn
changes every compass direction; a mirror changes only its named axis. Wait
and Use keep their meaning. **Reverse** reverses the order of all three cards.
The new loop starts at card 1 on the next turn, holding Pip still for this turn.
No keeper rewrite is spent or refilled. Repeated visits transform the new loop
again. A reverse can put Use first, causing a second activation before Pip leaves.

**Route hardware** shows each operation and its credited activation target.
Required targets appear in Quest; credits stop at the target while the prism
continues to work. Undo restores both cards and credits. Allowed dock returns
clear credits and keep the current program without refilling rewrites. Rehearse
for free before applying; deliberate blocked steps can position Use correctly.

In **Build an adventure → Puzzles → Pip compass prism**, choose an operation,
name and one-to-four activation target. Map rotations also turn mirror axes;
map reflections reverse clockwise/counterclockwise operations. During play,
a rotating room carries the prism while its compass rule stays fixed.

**Puzzle assemblies → Download puzzle** shares a reusable piece as a puzzle file.
Another player chooses **Import puzzle** in their assembly drawer. Imports append
a new slot; existing pieces and world drafts stay safe. Rotate/place the piece,
then playtest the whole adventure before sharing its playable file, code, link
or Storybook. Puzzle files contain terrain and objects, not a runnable mission.

## The Signal Orchard — v0.36

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Signal Orchard**.
Six chapters introduce signal stamps, then build toward two changing routes,
three courier pairs, eight deliveries and a three-bell duet.

A signal stamp has two engravings: **Quiet** and **Powered**. When Pip executes
Use on it, any linked lever on, or every linked plate held, selects Powered.
Otherwise it loads Quiet. The loaded loop stays unchanged when the signal
changes later. The next card starts at 1 on the following turn; loading costs
no keeper rewrite. Plan your return visit before changing the signal.

**Route hardware** lists both programs, the signal number, current power and
which routes you have loaded. A **both routes** quest needs a separate Use
under each signal. The keeper, crates and Pip can hold plates. Undo restores
programs and route records; allowed dock returns clear those records without
refilling rewrites. Use free rehearsal to preview the signal you currently hold.

In the creator, paint **Pip signal stamp** and at least one plate or lever on
its channel. Inspect to engrave both routes. Choose Quiet or Powered before
rotating directions or borrowing saved cards. Optional both-route goals invite
round trips. Capture the whole linked puzzle as an assembly, then playtest both
visits before sharing. The game does not upload your maps to a public server;
share the downloaded file, code, link or Storybook with your players.

## The Lantern Loom — v0.35

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Lantern Loom**.
Six chapters teach Pip to load programs from **M** stamps. Stand Pip on the
stamp, then execute **Use**. Its three cards replace the current loop; the next
turn starts at card 1. Loading holds Pip still and does not spend or refill
keeper rewrites. Walking over a stamp or using it with the keeper does not load it.

Required stamps appear in Quest. Read their engravings and loaded status under
Pip’s **Route hardware** disclosure. Rehearsal follows program changes without
changing your trail. Undo restores the old program and stamp records. Returning
to an allowed dock clears records and deliveries, keeps the current cards, and
does not refill the rewrite allowance. The Loom trials seal the dock.

To create a route, paint **Pip program stamp** in Puzzles and inspect it. Choose
three cards, give it a name, and decide whether it must be loaded to finish.
Rotate directions or borrow a saved satchel program for free, then **Apply object**.
Capture engravings as puzzle assemblies to reuse them. Test the whole return
trip before exporting a verified adventure file, code, play link or Storybook.

## The Midnight Parcelworks — v0.34

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Midnight Parcelworks**.
Five chapters introduce courier tubes, then combine them with limited rewrites,
ordered deliveries, bells and crossings that need both you and Pip.

A **U1** tube connects to the other **U1**, U2 to U2, and so on. Pip must stand
on a tube and execute **Use** to travel. Walking and Wait keep it in place.
A blocked landing refuses travel but advances the card; clear the landing and
try again. Only Pip uses these tubes. A delivery flag at the exit counts as
soon as Pip arrives. Pip’s **Courier tube directory** lists both coordinates.

**Remix the cards · free** edits your draft: Rotate turns directions clockwise,
Mirror swaps East/West, and Reverse reads the cards backward. Wait and Use keep
their meaning. Rehearsal, satchel loading and draft tools do not spend turns
or rewrites. **Apply** still does, and **Restore current cards** discards a draft.

Want to invent a route? Remix a Parcelworks world in Adventure Workshop, or
paint **Pip courier tube** from Puzzles. The brush pairs endpoints automatically;
inspect them to rename or change channels. Add flags, playtest the entire round
trip, then share a proved adventure file, code, play link or Storybook.

## The Brassbound Atlas — v0.33

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Brassbound Atlas**.
Four harder trials ask Pip to deliver flags in order while you arrange crossings
and restore your own light. Quest and Pip’s **Delivery manifest** list the route.
A later flag does not count before its turn. A final parking goal also needs Pip
to remain paused on that flag until you finish at the beacon.

Some trials limit **program rewrites**: every Apply uses one, including applying
unchanged cards. Start/Pause and single-card stepping use no rewrites. Undo can
recover a rewrite; Restart begins a fresh attempt. A sealed dock disables Return
to dock. In custom worlds where returning is allowed, it clears deliveries but
does not refill rewrites. Free rehearsal and **Restore current cards** let you
experiment without changing the trail.

**Your program satchel**, inside Pip’s workbench, holds eight named arrangements
per save journey. Select a pocket, name your chosen cards, and Save in slot.
Loading fills the card editor for free; Apply still follows the world’s rules.
Saving replaces that pocket; Undo satchel edit recovers the previous edit for
this visit. Download/import the satchel to share programs across browsers, or
use a complete game backup to retain it with your journey. It contains cards,
not a map or a suspended game.

## Pip’s Pocket Orchestra — v0.32

Open **Choose your adventure → Storybooks → Pip and the Pocket Orchestra**.
Six peaceful chapters teach remote switches, repeating bell songs, a player/Pip
duet, and crossings that need both of you. Optional treasure detours and chapter
promises offer more routes. The earlier Clockwork Post keeps its original levels.

A **Use** card operates the lever or bell on Pip’s exact tile, without reaching
adjacent tiles. Repeating Use can switch a lever off again. Pause or step through
the program to keep a crossing open. Wrong bell notes restart the melody; Quest
lists the order. A completed melody stays complete. Pip cannot collect lights,
push crates or open key gates.

**Advance one card** works while paused, spends one turn and leaves Pip paused.
Blocked or empty cards still advance. **Borrow a program recipe** fills your
cards for free. Apply edited cards before starting or stepping; Apply costs one
turn. **Rehearse six cards** changes no saves: it starts at card 1 from Pip’s
current position and holds you still. Moving yourself onto/off a plate later
can change the real route. Undo restores Pip, switches and melody together.

## New roads in v0.31

**Choose your adventure → Storybooks** opens complete tales directly from Play.
Search the shelves, use four-cover pages, or open Your story bookmarks. Every
book version and rule set keeps its completed chapters and branching choices.
The latest Continue trail keeps the exact board and Undo history. Older books
return to their bookmarked chapter; they may require restarting that chapter.

**The Clockwork Post** teaches a new companion in three peaceful chapters.
Pip follows three direction/Wait cards. Use Tools → Clockwork companion, or

## Choose your road

**[Open the game](https://youknowjp.github.io/Tilewright/#play)** in your
browser; no installation or account is needed. On an already open copy, choose
**Play adventures / Main menu**. The title screen lets
you look around before starting a mission. **How to play** introduces movement,
objectives, and specialist tools. **Options** controls sound, reading size, and
motion. Sound starts only when you choose it.

New here? Choose **Training camp · Start here**. Follow Mira in **First Light
Crossing** to learn walking, conversations, a lever crossing, lanterns, and
bringing a keeper home. The second lesson helps you paint and write a real
adventure, finish its playtest, and keep or share it. The current step stays
above the map on phones. Practice changes do not affect your existing worlds
or game progress. [Read about the two lessons](TRAINING.md).

| Road | What awaits |
| --- | --- |
| Story chapters | Twenty-four authored missions in eight regions. Begin any chapter; finish its missions in order. |
| Wayfarers’ Guild | Eight jobs with three specialists: halt hunters, veil your movement, or leave a lantern double. |
| Expeditions | Seeded journeys, daily trails, camps, upgrades, and route choices. Saved checkpoints let you return later. |
| Adventure Workshop | Twenty-seven featured worlds, player-made adventures, and tools to create/remix/share your own. |
| Terrain Mapmaker | Paint the land first, then explore it or build an authored adventure from it. |

Main menu preserves the current trail while you browse. **Continue** returns to
that trail. **Adventure journal** shows story stars, Guild honors, expedition
badges, and suggestions. After a reload, **Continue trail** restores the exact
latest adventure and up to 60 recent Undo actions. Use **Undo · Z** to try another
answer, including after a loss. Completed rewards and camp transitions cannot
be undone. Training keeps its own practice saves. [Saving guide](CONTINUE.md).

## Bring back the light

Restore every golden lantern, finish the mission’s extra goals, then reach the
turquoise beacon. Read **Quest & map → Quest** for the current story and goals.
Messages stay beneath the board until your next action.

Move with arrows/WASD or the direction pad. Swipes also move on touch screens.
Shift + direction dashes two tiles using energy. Space waits. The world advances
when you spend a turn; reading a conversation or the free **Plan** forecast
spends none. P pauses; R restarts; H opens a scout hint.

Wardens mark their next step. Hunters respond to sight, sound, and learned
movement patterns. Change direction, walk through ferns, use Q for a decoy, or
C for a shield. B uses a specialist ability when available. **Plan** explains
nearby threats before you commit.

E uses a nearby action: talk, ring a bell, operate a lever, take a relic, open
supplies, or travel through a waystone. When several actions are nearby, choose
the intended target first. Keepers’ advice stays under Notes. A singing relic
must be carried home; X sets it down, and E takes it again.

Trail is the normal challenge. Nemesis has fewer supplies and stronger hunters.
Bests are kept separately. A clean route within the mission’s target earns three
stars; treasure adds points. There is no real-time countdown.

## Make a place worth following

Mapmaker is the terrain table. Select a tile, choose Brush/Fill/Rectangle, and
paint. The selected layer determines where it goes. **Fit map** shows everything;
zoom and use Pan for detailed work. On compact screens, **Tile palette** and
**Layers & world** open separately so the map keeps its space. Undo/Redo also
restore layer and resize changes. Export JSON to keep an editable terrain backup
or PNG for a picture.

**Build adventure** copies terrain into a new Workshop draft. It leaves the
original map alone. Or open the Workshop to start blank, use one of twenty
region/mission recipes, or remix an existing world. The **Creator’s guide**
explains the whole path in three illustrated chapters.

In the World studio, quick brushes handle common objects; **Brushes** opens the
full palette. The objective strip shows counts and selects goal brushes. Inspect
an object to edit its dialogue, channel, or route. World notes group the title,
invitation, toolkit, story, and challenge settings. Start small: one beginning,
one lantern, one interesting crossing, and a way home.

Want something to tinker with? **Workshop → Puzzle Lab** has twelve complete rooms
to play or remix, plus design sparks for your next idea. **Remix tools** turns
or mirrors your whole world in one undoable step. Move the editor cursor and
choose **Practise from cursor** to try a particular area without recording a
best or changing a creator clear. Full Playtest proves the route from the real
starting point. [Explore the Lab](PUZZLE_LAB.md).

**Checks** finds missing objectives and broken links. Finish **Playtest** to
attach a real creator clear, then **Share** a file or code. Your friend imports
it through **Workshop → Import & play**. Publicly hosted copies can also make
smaller play links. A local computer’s address cannot serve as a public play
link. See [the Workshop guide](WORKSHOP.md) for object rules and limits.

For a longer road, **Workshop → Storybooks** includes two new four-chapter
stories with eight original levels, plus three journeys through Puzzle Lab.
[Meet the keepers and new chapters](NEW_STORIES.md). Read each scene, choose an optional promise, and turn the page after
finishing. Missing an honor never blocks your story. Bind two to six of your
own worlds, write scenes, and share a `.storybook.json` file. Camps retain
completed chapters; **Resume chapter** restores the exact latest adventure after
reload. [Storybooks guide](STORYBOOKS.md).

## Keep the stories you make

Open **Options → Saved data** to download or restore the complete game in one
backup file. To begin again with no saved progress, worlds, drafts, habits, or
options, choose **Start a fresh game…** and confirm the erasure. Cancel preserves
the current game. Download a backup first to keep it. [Saved-data guide](SAVED_DATA.md).

Maps, drafts, shelves, and bests stay in this browser. Keep downloaded backups
before clearing browser data or moving devices. A saving warning means your
current visit can continue, but you should export what you want to keep.
Expeditions have separate checkpoint backups; the Workshop has separate adventure
files. Imported adventures preview before playing or adding to your shelf.

The game runs in your browser with portable sharing. It does not require an
account. Structural checks cannot guarantee every puzzle choice is solvable;
playtest your outward and return routes before asking a friend to follow them.

## A clearer board and eighteen more adventures

The header’s house returns to the main menu and its corner icon toggles full
screen. The dock keeps nearby interaction, Wait, Dash, and your specialist or
partner action beside the movement pad. **Tools** holds supplies, inventory,
control choices, and Options. **Quest** holds story, objectives, and the written
bell order. **Plan** previews the next move without spending a turn. Long trail
messages offer **Read more** in a separate reading window.

Find three new six-chapter books through **Workshop → Storybooks → New
adventures**. Play **The Weatherkeepers’ Almanac** for river puzzles and a
rainseed rescue, **The Night Post** for longer deliveries and optional heists,
or **The Open Workshop** for peaceful two-traveler inventions. Every chapter
can also be played or remixed through **Player adventures → New adventures**.
See [screen view and new adventures](SCREEN_AND_SAGAS.md).


After a finish, **Quest & notes** lets you reread your objectives, map, and
keeper journal before choosing the next adventure. The pause screen offers
the same action. Reading preserves the result, and Back returns to its opener.

### Borrowed beats and chosen roads

In Pip & Sprout, choose **The Bellkeeper** for four timed adventures. Use a numbered **Timed bell A** to power matching bridges and signals starting on the next tick. Use **One tick** or pause the run to inspect at your own pace. Its countdown shows how many powered ticks remain; an occupied bridge stays safe until its courier leaves. Some stories require both home flags and complete silence before ending.

Creators can set one to six powered ticks on a bell, capture it in a blueprint, and add a silent-bell finish goal. In the collection builder’s **Curator tray**, choose chapters from the assembly or a saved collection. Add independent copies together, undo that batch, or share only the selected chapters as a file, link or poster. Original maker credits travel with the maps; your saved attempts and clears stay private in this journey.

### Passport roads and optional ribbons

Try **The Sealkeepers** in Pip & Sprout. A **Parcel seal G** checks who is entering and what they carry. An inspection arrow checks only that travel direction; other directions pass freely. The seal keeps the parcel. Read the tile or blocked-entry message, pause, undo and inspect at your own pace.

Collection creators can add optional tick and placed-part limits under a chapter’s **Optional bonus goal**, or copy a verified creator answer as its par. Clearing the chapter always counts. A bonus ribbon rewards one answer meeting all selected limits, and a later slower retry keeps an earned ribbon. Bonus details stay folded in the chapter menu. Share goal editions, selected chapters and posters using the same collection tools; your personal progress stays in this journey and its backups.
